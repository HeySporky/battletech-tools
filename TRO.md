# Technical Readout: House Customs

Equipment we designed ourselves, in the style of a Technical Readout. Customs taken from someone else, such as
fan-made books or the video games, go under "Others' customs" with their source.

Every entry is Custom (rules level 6) unless it says otherwise. The rulings behind each one are in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-beta-feedback-design.md`.

## House customs

### Long Tom Artillery: Anti-Orbital Ammo

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Tech base** | Inner Sphere |
| **Rules level** | Custom |
| **Tech rating** | E |
| **Prototype** | 3055 (Federated Suns) |
| **Introduced** | 3062 |
| **Weight** | 1 ton (3 shots) |
| **Damage** | 2 capital (the Long Tom's 25 standard is 2.5, rounded down) |
| **Cost** | 60,000 C-bills a ton |
| **Battle Value** | 28 a ton |
| **Explosive** | Yes |

**Overview:** On 7 May 3050 the Smoke Jaguar WarShip *Sabre Cat* razed the city of Edo on Turtle Bay from orbit.
The Federated Suns resolved that it would never happen to one of their worlds. Their answer was a shell that a field
Long Tom could fire into orbit. A prototype fired in 3055, and the round was in production by 3062. It was fielded
against the Word of Blake's fleets in the Jihad.

**Capabilities:** Each round has three stages. An overcharged propellant charge throws it clear of the tube, and a
rocket sustainer carries it up out of the atmosphere. The last stage is a small semi-guided payload that steers onto
the target. The round is too small for most defenses to track. The extra propellant and the guided stage take up
room in the shell. As a result, it hits a little lighter than the high-explosive round, and a ton holds only 3
rounds, against 5 for the standard round. Each round also costs twice as much as a Copperhead.

**Game rules:** The round fires a surface-to-orbit attack under the Strategic Operations rules for capital and
sub-capital weapons (StratOps pp.91, 97-99), as a ballistic attack. A hit does 2 points of capital-scale damage.
It loads in any Long Tom. These house rules add to the StratOps rules (user rulings 2026-10-22):

- **Rate of fire:** The round has a long way to travel, and a turn in space is longer than a ground turn:
  6 ground turns pass for each space turn, and the gun fires once a space turn. Between shots the breech cools and
  is inspected, and the gun loads the extra propellant.
- **Firing early:** Until the next space turn, the Long Tom cannot safely fire any round, ground artillery included.
  A crew that fires early anyway rolls 2D6 first. On 2-4 the gun fails catastrophically: it is destroyed and
  inoperable for the rest of the game. On 5-8 it misfires. On 9-12 it fires normally.
- **Misfire:** The round goes wild. Roll 1D6 for its direction from the aimed hex on the Scatter Diagram (TacOps
  p.182), then 3D6 for how many hexes it travels (the Advanced Scatter roll for the worst misses). It goes off where
  it lands for its normal damage, hitting whatever is there, friendly units included. If it lands off the playing
  area, it is lost.
- **Reach:** The target must be in the first section of the space/atmospheric map. Anything farther out is out of
  reach.
- **Moving targets:** The round flies to the hex the target was in when it fired. If the target has moved one hex
  out of that hex, the round can still hit, but it is unlikely, because space is vast: make the normal attack roll
  at +4 to the to-hit number. Two hexes or more is a miss.
- **Bystanders:** Anything else that has moved into the aimed hex may be hit instead, on the normal attack roll.
- **Defenses:** The round is too small to target well. Fighters and point defense are rarely any use against it,
  but even blind squirrels find nuts now and then: they stop it only on a natural 12.

In Play Mode, turn on "Space forces in this game" on the roster page to track the space turns and each shot.

**Battle Value:** The standard round is 46 a ton, about 9.2 a shot. 3 shots come to 27.6, rounded up to 28. Its
reach to orbit adds no range on the ground map, so range does not change the figure.

## Others' customs

### Hailstorm Artillery

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Source** | MechWarrior 5: Mercenaries and Clans (video games) |
| **Tech base** | Inner Sphere and Clan |
| **Rules level** | Apocryphal |
| **Tech rating** | B, as the Long Tom |
| **Prototype** | 2445 |
| **Introduced** | 2500 |
| **Weight** | 25 tons, towed only |
| **Damage** | 35 to the target hex; 25, 15 and 5 to the rings around it |
| **Range** | 12 mapsheets |
| **Ammunition** | 4 shots a ton |
| **Cost** | 375,000 C-bills; standard rounds 11,200 a ton |
| **Battle Value** | 515; standard rounds 52 a ton |

**Overview:** In the MechWarrior 5 games, Hailstorm batteries shell 'Mechs from behind the lines. The games give
no tabletop stats, so the figures above are ours. The Hailstorm is a heavy, short-barreled tube on a towed carriage.
It hits harder than the Long Tom, but it reaches less than half as far.

**Capabilities:** It mounts only on a Support Vehicle trailer, which a tractor pulls into position. It fires the
munitions the Long Tom, Sniper and Thumper all share, in its own caliber: high-explosive, cluster, Copperhead, FAE,
FASCAM, flechette, illumination, smoke and Thunder.

**Game rules:** It fires as the Long Tom does, under the artillery rules (TO:AUE p.96). Each round uses the Long
Tom round's rules. Where a round's damage follows the Long Tom's 25, the Hailstorm does 35 instead; that is our
provisional reading.

**How we derived it:** The cost is 15,000 C-bills a ton, the rate for the Long Tom and Sniper. The Battle Value is the
Long Tom's 368 × 35/25. Each round costs and counts per shot as the Long Tom's round × 35/25. A ton holds 4 shots
against the Long Tom's 5, so a ton comes to 1.12 times the Long Tom's ton.

**Credit:** Added at the user's request (user rulings 2026-10-22).
