# Technical Readout: Future Wars DropShips

Four ways to build a DropShip from *Future Wars*, a BattleTech fanzine of 1989-1993, written before *TechManual*
gave DropShips construction rules of their own. Each is a Special Ruleset in the Small Craft Creator (Custom Homebrew,
rules level 6): tick one on a DropShip and the creator weighs the ship by that author's rules instead of
*TechManual*'s. Only one can be ticked at a time. Everything built under them is marked NOT OFFICIAL.

DropShips are a unit type Catalyst Game Labs publishes, so this readout covers the construction methods and their
sample designs, not a new kind of unit.

The four authors, and where each method was printed:

- **Mark Stock**, "Dropship Construction", *Future Wars* #16 (1990), p.32, with his Pioneer on pp.34-36. Reprinted in
  *The Best of Future Wars, Volume One* (1991), p.7, with the same rules in a rewritten introduction, so the app
  offers one ruleset for both printings.
- **Erik Eid**, "Deluxe Dropship Construction", *Future Wars* #25 (1991), pp.6-7, built on Stock's.
- **Mike Reese**, "More Drop Ship Construction" and "BattleTech Drop Ship Construction", *Future Wars* #36 (1993),
  pp.14-18, with the UNION-12, THUNDERWOLF and GOLIATH-R sheets on pp.19-21. Issue #36 prints no year; it reviews
  *BattleSpace* (FASA, 1993) and carries art signed September 1992.
- **Doug Houseman**, "Designing a Dropship", *Future Wars* #36 (1993), pp.22-23. He wrote it in 1991, "before the
  new rules were released".

The rules texts are not reproduced here: they belong to their authors. This page says what each method does and
where NGCFells had to fill a gap. Each gap NGCFells filled is marked *Inspired by* the author, and any fault in it
lies with NGCFells.

## The Four Methods

*Mark Stock, Erik Eid, Mike Reese and Doug Houseman, Future Wars #16, #25 and #36.*

### Stock

The engine weighs the tonnage divided by (15 / thrust), and the structure Structural Integrity x tonnage / 100, with
no limit on the integrity. Fuel is carried by the ton. Bays weigh 150 tons a cubicle and 5 tons a door, vehicles 100
or 50 tons, and people 2 to 10 tons each. The rest of the ship is cargo. Four values are to be found "by looking in
DS/JS under a ship of equal type and tonnage": thrust points a ton of fuel, tons burned a day, the crew (which sets
the bridge, 1 to 10 times the crew) and armor points a ton.

### Eid

Stock's method made more exact. The engine is tonnage / (16 / thrust), and overthrust is 1.5 times thrust. The
integrity must be above the overthrust. Four charts give thrust points a ton, the engine's free heat sinks, and armor
points a ton for an aerodyne and for a spheroid. The bridge is 0.0075 of the tonnage, and doors weigh nothing (at
most eight). Eid lists the advanced equipment a DropShip may not carry, and leaves the cost to the builder.

### Reese

Eid's charts, extended to the smallest and largest ships. A ship carries 20 to 30 days of fuel, so his ships are
heavy. The integrity runs from the overthrust to three times it, and the bridge is at most 25 tons. The engine may be
split among several engines, each armored. A Clan ship's XL engine weighs half, and its weapons fire with "a +2 bonus
to hit". He also gives a way to find a ship's tonnage from a 1/285 model's volume, and the creator has a calculator
for it.

### Houseman

A percent-of-mass method. Each point of integrity is 3% of the ship's mass, 10 internal structure, 20 armor a facing
and 1 g. The hull is 5% (spheroid) or 10% (aerodyne), internal power 2%, landing gear 10% or 15%, and communications
and controls 3%. Thrusters weigh 3 tons a point of thrust for each 100 tons, and fuel 3 tons a point of thrust a day.
Weapons weigh half again their FASA weight. His bays are 200% of what they carry and his cargo space 110% of the
cargo. Armor, landing, communications, quarters, weapons, bays, launch tubes and recycling together must stay under
25% of the ship.

## What NGCFells Filled In

*NGCFells, Inspired by Stock, Eid, Reese and Houseman.*

- **Stock's "look in DS/JS".** NGCFells: "Lets do the work for them. Set up formulae based on weight, type, etc."
  Each value is a formula fitted to every DropShip in *DropShips and JumpShips* (FASA, 1988), shown in the table
  below with each ship's printed value. The builder may still set any of them by hand. Stock's heat-sink divisor
  (10-12 for small engines, 13-16 medium, 17 and up large) names no sizes. It runs from 10 for the smallest DS&JS
  engine to 25 for the largest, on a log scale.
- **Houseman's integrity table** prints rows 1 to 8. NGCFells extended it with no cap, at 3% of mass, 10 internal
  structure, 20 armor and 1 g a point, "as large as needed to cover all dropships of whatever size". A build whose
  percentages pass 100% of its mass is an issue, not a cap.
- **Houseman's heat sinks.** He gives no rule: a heat sink weighs a ton, and none are free.
- **Houseman's fuel.** He gives no thrust points a ton. NGCFells reads his 3 tons a thrust point a day against Reese's
  day at 1 g (1,440 thrust points) as 480 thrust points a ton.
- **Battle Value and cost.** None of the four gives a Battle Value. Both are worked by the rules edition's DropShip
  rules on the design as built. Eid's own word on cost is printed with his designs: "typical values range from 30
  million C-Bills for a Fury to 2 billion C-Bills for a Behemoth"; salvaged ships cost 40% of new, destroyed ones half
  of salvaged.
- **Fire factors.** Stock, Eid and Reese print fire factors per arc: the arc's maximum damage over 10, rounding up
  from .6 (Eid, FW #25 p.6; Reese, FW #36 p.16). Eid adds 1 for each +4 of to-hit bonus in the arc. The record sheet
  prints them in its notes.

## Their Own Arithmetic

*Mike Reese, Future Wars #36, pp.16-18.*

Reese's worked examples carry slips, which the creator does not copy. His book Union, built to his rules, comes to
8,487 tons, not the 8,437 he prints: he leaves out the 40 tons of armor and counts 14 pilots at 5 tons as 60. His
UNION-12 comes to 20,436 tons before its cargo and carried units, not 20,426, from the same pilots' slip. He tells the
reader to use a pi of 3.145, but his own UNION-12 globe was worked with the true pi: by his rule a 12-inch globe makes
22,885 tons, where his worked example gets 22,860. The UNION-12 sample keeps his 22,860.

## Canon and the Methods

*NGCFells's verification builds, from DropShips and JumpShips (FASA, 1988), pp.38-43.*

None of the four methods rebuilds a canon DropShip at its canon tonnage. Each verification build takes the Leopard,
Union or Overlord exactly as *DropShips and JumpShips* prints it (weapons, ammunition, armor, cubicles, crew and
pilots), weighs it by one method and keeps the canon tonnage. Stock and Eid come closest. Reese's 20 to 30 days of
fuel put thousands of tons on every ship, and Houseman's percentages overshoot most. The methods are built as their authors wrote them, not tuned to canon. Each
build is a sample in the Small Craft Creator.
